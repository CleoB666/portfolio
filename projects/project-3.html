Duro: Website Usability and Accessibility Review

A three-week review of Duro’s website using usability testing and WCAG level AA criteria to guide design recommendations.

WCAG AA Accessibility User Testing UX/UI Design
Role

UX/UI Designer

Collaboration

Duro

Project Type

Accessibility Review

Duration

3 weeks

Focus

WCAG AA
Usability

Methods

User Testing
UX Analysis

Challenge

Finding the obstacles in a familiar website

My aim was to understand where people struggled to find information and what made the site harder to use. I focused on navigation, content structure and accessibility.

Research

What testing showed

Watching people navigate the website revealed places where labels were unclear and important content was difficult to find. Some pages also asked users to work through too much information at once.

Approach

Turning observations into recommendations

I brought the usability findings together with a review against WCAG level AA criteria. The resulting suggestions focused on clearer menus, stronger hierarchy, contrast and more readable content.

Outcome

A proposal, not a claim of compliance

I developed design recommendations and a concept for clearer navigation and content. These were proposals, not changes to the live website or proof of WCAG compliance.

Behind the Design

From review to recommendations

I combined what I saw in testing with accessibility criteria, then developed design ideas that could be tested and refined.

WCAG Analysis

Reviewing contrast, navigation and content structure using WCAG level AA criteria as a reference.

User Testing

Watching where people hesitated, lost their place or struggled to find information.

Design Improvements

Suggestions for simpler menus, clearer hierarchy and better visual guidance.

Final Concept

A proposed direction for making the site easier to understand and navigate.

Key Learnings

Accessibility is usability

WCAG criteria gave me a more reliable starting point than my own impression of what looked readable.

User testing reveals hidden friction

Seeing someone search for an answer often reveals problems that are easy to miss when you already know the site.

Clarity reduces cognitive load

Small decisions about labels, spacing and hierarchy can affect how confidently people find their way.

Reflection

What the review taught me

Accessibility became much less abstract when I could connect a guideline to something a person actually struggled with.

I also practised turning technical criteria into recommendations that made sense for Duro’s website and its users.
