ViLike: Vili's Active Adventure

A five-week UX/UI concept developed with ViLike, exploring how to make everyday movement feel more like play.

Gamification Motivation Child UX Behaviour Design
Role

UX/UI Designer

Collaboration

ViLike, Finland

Tool

Figma

Duration

5 weeks

Focus

Gamification
Child-centred UX

Challenge

Making movement feel like an invitation

The brief was to encourage children to be more active. I kept coming back to one question: how could the experience invite them to move without making it feel like another task?

Approach

Exploring progress, play and feedback

I mapped the journey, sketched different ideas and developed prototype screens in Figma. I wanted the rewards to be easy to understand without taking attention away from the activity itself.

Outcome

A concept, ready for further testing

The prototype brings together visible progress, feedback and rewards. It shows how the experience could work, but whether it changes everyday habits is a question for longer-term testing.

Behind the Design

From the first questions to a prototype

I moved from assumptions and quick sketches to a user journey and a clickable prototype. Each stage helped me decide what the child would see, do and understand next.

Crazy 8 Ideation

Quick sketches to explore different ways of turning movement into play.

Assumptions & HMW

Writing down assumptions and turning them into questions worth exploring.

User Journey

Mapping a child's steps through the experience, including possible moments of confusion or delight.

Prototype

A clickable concept showing the main flow, progression and rewards.

Key Learnings

Gamification

Feedback makes progress visible. The harder design decision is deciding when a reward supports the activity and when it becomes a distraction.

Child-centred Design

With younger users in mind, clear choices and timely feedback matter more than adding extra features.

Industry Collaboration

Working with ViLike meant considering the brief alongside the experience I wanted children to have.

Reflection

What I would test next

The next step I would want is to watch children use the prototype. What feels playful to a designer may not feel the same to a child, and that is worth finding out.

The collaboration also gave me practice explaining why a design choice belonged in the experience, rather than relying on how appealing it looked.
