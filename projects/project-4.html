Background

A studio built around ideas we share

My sister and I started Sirius Entertainment so we could build our ideas together. One part of that work is The Tiny Heist Crew, a browser game we have developed, tested and continued to refine. Other projects are at earlier stages.

Approach

Connecting the story to what players do

My work covers player flows, interfaces, gameplay structure, narrative planning and prototypes. I think about what players need to understand in a moment and how the game can communicate it without interrupting play.

Product Direction

Different products, different stages

Alongside our browser game, we are developing ideas for a PC game and a separate mobile storytelling app. Neither is presented here as a finished release; both have their own design and development challenges.

Current Status

Sharing the work without sharing everything

The studio is still growing, and our projects are not all at the same stage. This case study shares my role and design process while keeping unreleased story and game details private.

Key Learnings

Product vision

Building several projects at once has taught me to give each one a clear purpose rather than forcing them into the same mould.

Game UX

A game needs to communicate through the experience itself. Timing, feedback and readable choices matter as much as the interface.

Protecting ideas

I can show my process and decisions without revealing stories and concepts that are still being developed.

Reflection

What building a studio has taught me

Building our own projects means thinking beyond the next screen. The world, the mechanics and the user experience have to make sense together.

It also means making real choices about scope: what we can build now, what we need to test and what can wait.
